What to Do When a Pipe Bursts in Milwaukee, WI

Home / Blog / What to Do When a Pipe Bursts in Milwaukee, WI

A burst pipe is one of the most stressful plumbing emergencies a homeowner can experience. At 4 to 8 gallons every minute, one broken pipe can soak whole rooms, damage what you own, and compromise the structure in a matter of hours. Act correctly in those opening minutes and you may spare yourself thousands of dollars in losses, turning a potential major restoration job into a repair you can manage. Below, Milwaukee Restoration Co lays out a step by step plan that homeowners in Milwaukee, WI can follow when it happens.

Step 1: Turn the Water Off Right Away

Before anything else, cut off the water, because nothing you do afterward matters as much as stopping the flow quickly. Damage and repair bills grow with each minute the leak runs. Here's what to do: locate your main water shutoff valve. In most Milwaukee homes, it sits near the water meter, typically in the garage, basement, utility room, or along an exterior wall near the street. Rotating it clockwise, the old righty-tighty rule, cuts water to the whole home. When the broken pipe is easy to spot and has a dedicated valve close by, shut that one too.

Can't find the valve, or can't get it to turn? Phone the water utility right away and ask them to stop service at the meter. Knowing where that valve lives ahead of time, not during a crisis, matters for exactly this reason. Take a few minutes today to locate yours and make sure it operates smoothly. If it's stuck, have a plumber service it before you need it in an emergency.

Step 3: Protect Your Property and Belongings

While waiting for professional help, take these steps to minimize damage: move valuable items, electronics, and documents away from wet areas. Slip plastic blocks or aluminum foil beneath furniture legs so nothing sits directly on soaked carpet. Pick up any area rugs lying on wet flooring. Where puddles are easy to get to, blot them up with towels. Open closet and cabinet doors to promote air circulation. Cut power to wet areas at the breaker box, but only when that is safe and you are not standing in water.

Standing water around appliances, panels, or electrical outlets is off limits, so stay out of it. Can't get to the breaker panel safely because of serious flooding? Leave the power as it is until trained help arrives and assesses things. Safety always comes first.

Step 4: Document Everything for Insurance

Your insurance claim will need evidence, so record everything carefully before any cleaning starts. Take photos and video of all standing water, damaged materials, and affected belongings. If the spot where the pipe failed is visible, record it, along with the day and approximate hour you first noticed the problem. Milwaukee Restoration Co supplies detailed reports as well, and paired with your notes they strengthen your claim and your case for fair payment.

Lowering Your Burst Pipe Risk in Milwaukee

Some bursts cannot be avoided, but Milwaukee homeowners lower the odds a great deal when they insulate exposed pipes in unheated spots such as attics, crawl spaces, and garages. When temperatures drop, let faucets drip a little so water keeps moving in the lines. For sinks on outside walls, leave the cabinet doors beneath them open so warm air gets to the plumbing. Keep an eye on water pressure, and if it runs above 80 PSI, have a pressure reducing valve put in. Replace aging supply lines, especially rubber washing machine hoses, with braided stainless steel. And know your shutoff valve location before you need it.
